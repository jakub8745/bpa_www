For quite some time, I struggled to update our gallery site—mainly due to persistent hosting issues. Things aren’t quite so straightforward when you’re running everything through IPFS. Now, though, I finally have full access again. The site code has been dramatically simplified—admittedly, I’d come to rely far too much on 'intelligence'. The trouble with intelligence, though, is that it has a curious tendency to follow the principle of “horror vacui”—the fear of empty space, the compulsion to fill every gap with something, anything. And yet, as so often proves true, the simplest solutions are usually the best.

There’s another change worth mentioning: I’ve shifted my approach to the Gallery Archive. For the time being, the 'metaverse' version is postponed; that’s a project for another day. Doing all this solo means the whole process of coding and testing the archive locked me up for nearly a year. Now, I’m programming a new interface, decoupled from the decentralised gallery system. It’s based on configuration files stored on IPFS, from which each exhibition is dynamically built. I’m making use of various modules—this will let me plug in all sorts of applications to the interface, not just exhibition spaces but other experimental projects too, such as 'Milkmaid’s Pitcher' or more immersive documentation like the 'Bednarczyk' project space.

Some projects have already migrated to the new archive. You can have a look at a test version [here](link). The priority now is to transfer all the 'traditional' exhibitions. In the meantime, I’ll be extending the interface to support VR viewing, and more advanced XR applications will come later. Unfortunately, it seems we’ll be limited to Android devices for the time being, as Apple continues to block immersive sessions.

The next major task is integrating the archive with a wallet (e.g., MetaMask), since exhibition configuration files will be minted as NFTs. I’m still puzzling over how to make the most of free IPFS hosting. The loop seems to be tightening, and everything is getting more expensive—even Web3 isn’t immune.
